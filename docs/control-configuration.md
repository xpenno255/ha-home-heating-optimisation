# Control configuration

Home Assistant exposes **Settings → Devices & services → Home Heating Optimisation → Configure → Comfort and boiler control**. A new standalone setup walks through house/shared sources, boiler sources and limits, then every observed room. Existing control setups provide separate menu entries for those three areas.

This release configures the comfort and boiler engines as a pair. Each engine can be disabled independently, as can each room and its occupancy offsets, but both configurations must be complete. Required fields are the control survey and shared outdoor/flow sources, the boiler setpoint actuator and outdoor source, and each room's thermostat and survey room ID. The forms also expose bounded heating/DHW limits and explicit manual-change hold periods.

Observation and control mappings have different jobs. An observation air sensor supplies reports and analytics. A control air sensor is an explicit override for comfort decisions; when omitted, control uses the survey's preferred air source and then its thermostat fallbacks. The control sensor reports the source actually selected. Editing an observation source never silently changes its control counterpart, and both forms show the other selection. Controlled thermostats cannot be removed through observation options, although additional observation-only rooms can be added. The control survey directory must be changed explicitly in control options before its observation mapping is changed.

Standalone setup has no dependency on OT Thermostat Control or Boiler Flow Control. It creates the same schema used by an import, with empty legacy entry IDs and independent controller storage. Imported configurations keep their provenance, source fingerprint, rollback snapshot, learned seeds and live persistent controller state when edited. Selected room-air sensors are retained unless that room's control form explicitly changes them.

Every configuration save leaves the engines in shadow and clears persisted active modes. Editing is refused while a live or persisted room mode is `active`, or the boiler mode is `auto`. Rebinding the boiler setpoint actuator or a room thermostat resets ownership to `unclaimed`; run **Hand over controllers / Claim ownership** again after reviewing shadow output. Source and tuning edits made in shadow keep completed ownership, but do not activate either engine.

Initial setup never grants actuator ownership. For standalone setups, the handover action checks that no enabled legacy controller conflicts exist, journals ownership, and leaves all modes in shadow. For imports it additionally verifies and disables the retained legacy entries. Activation remains a separate, supervised step; use each room's mode select and the boiler mode select only after shadow comparison.

## DHW target schedule

**Configure → DHW target schedule** sets a normal cylinder target plus a higher target for a short local-time window on selected weekdays (default 50°C normal, 60°C higher, 04:00–06:00, no weekdays). It is off by default and independent of the comfort and boiler engines' modes.

Evohome keeps its DHW schedule and decides when the cylinder heats. HHO only changes the cylinder setpoint through `ramses_cc.set_dhw_params`, always resending the current overrun and differential. It never uses DHW mode, boost, reset or schedule services and never forces a charge.

Fields: the RAMSES water heater, a physical DHW-demand source (relay or demand sensor, not the water heater's on/off state), a measured cylinder-temperature source, the two targets (35–85°C in 0.5°C steps, higher above normal), weekdays and the window (start before end, same day). Enabling requires the water heater to report its current parameters. With no weekdays selected nothing is raised.

Enabling, or changing the normal target, permits one write of the normal target if the controller differs. Other saves and restarts do not repeat it. When enabled, the boiler engine reads its cylinder target from the selected water heater and marks it unconfirmed while a change is pending.

How a session runs:

- At window start on a selected day, if handover is complete, no automation or script changes that water heater's target temperature, no temporary DHW override (such as a boost) is active and the target is the normal value, HHO records the date and its intent, then raises the target. Automations that only switch DHW on, off or to boost are allowed. A boost active at window start delays the raise until it ends, within the window. Starting HA mid-window skips that day. Each date is used at most once, including across DST changes.
- It restores the normal target early once demand was seen, the measured temperature reached the higher target and demand has stayed off for 10 continuous minutes, or when Evohome's schedule turns DHW off after charging. A water heater mode that reads inactive while a charge is running is treated as stale and ignored. Otherwise it restores at window end. Outcomes are `complete`, `incomplete`, `no_charge`, `target_not_reached`, `insufficient_evidence` or `interrupted`.
- A target changed by anyone else is left alone: the schedule pauses (`paused`) until it is disabled and re-enabled or the normal target changes.
- Unload, reload or restart restores an owned raised target first and never re-raises that session. Recovery still runs if the feature was disabled or removed.

`sensor.home_heating_optimisation_dhw_schedule` shows the state (`disabled`, `armed`, `elevated`, `restoring`, `recovery_pending`, `paused`, `storage_read_only`, `misconfigured`), targets, next window, charge evidence and last outcome; the control report includes the same under `dhw_schedule`.

Limits: HA shows the controller's reply about a minute after a change, and a successful service call is not RF confirmation. RAMSES offers no compare-and-set, so a same-value manual change or a simultaneous external write cannot always be detected. Nothing can restore the target while HA or the RF link is down. Unconfirmed restores are retried a few times, then raise a repair and retry slowly. This is target scheduling only; it does not guarantee thermal disinfection or legionella protection. UK HSE guidance is to store hot water at 60°C or above.

## DHW measured-temperature cutoff

**Configure → DHW measured-temperature cutoff** stops a stored hot water charge from Home Assistant's own cylinder reading. It is off by default and independent of the DHW target schedule and the engines' modes.

Why: Evohome switches DHW from the last cylinder temperature the controller received. If it misses the sensor's reports just above its target, it keeps charging until the sensor next reports. Once the temperature settles that can be an hour later, so the cylinder overshoots (live: about 61 °C for a 50 °C target). HA's gateways often hear the sensor better than the controller does.

How it acts:

- Only while the water heater follows its schedule, the demand entity shows a charge, and HA's cylinder reading is under 10 minutes old and at or above the Evohome target plus the margin (default 1 °C). Boosts and manual overrides are left alone.
- It sends `ramses_cc.set_dhw_mode` with a 60-minute temporary override, DHW off. The controller ends that override by itself, so HA stopping or restarting never leaves DHW off longer than the remaining hour.
- While HA still measures the cylinder at or above the controller's reheat point (target minus differential), the hold is renewed shortly before it ends, up to 4 hours in total. Once HA measures it below that point, DHW is handed back to the schedule (`follow_schedule`), so the next charge starts on the controller's own terms.
- With the optional **Evohome cloud water heater** set, it also stays out of the way while that entity shows an override or the schedule has DHW off. Its mode survives an HA restart, when the RAMSES entity can briefly show `follow_schedule` in the middle of a boost.
- A stop the controller does not echo within 3 minutes is sent once more, then dropped until that charge ends. Automations or scripts that write the DHW target block it, as for the target schedule.
- On a higher-target day the cutoff follows that higher target.

`sensor.home_heating_optimisation_dhw_cutoff` shows `watching`, `holding`, `unconfirmed` or `standby`, with the current cutoff temperature, hold end and last action. Every stop, renewal and release is journalled (`dhw_cutoff`).

## Optimum start predictor (shadow)

**Configure → Optimum start predictor (shadow)** is off by default and shadow only: it never writes to a device and does not change any controller's mode. Its only service call is the read-only `weather.get_forecasts`. It needs historical analytics.

Why: Evohome's Optimum Start is the only thing that pre-heats a room before an upward switchpoint (HHO releases the zone and lets Evohome decide), and Optimum Stop was turned off after it silently dropped a room to 18 °C. This builds HHO's own start/stop logic in shadow so it can be scored against Evohome before any release acts on it.

- **Learning.** A warm-up event starts when a room's target rises by at least 0.5 K while the room is at least 0.5 K short, and succeeds once the room is within the comfort tolerance of that target for 10 minutes (later 0.1–0.3 K target nudges are ignored; the event is abandoned if the target drops 0.3 K below the goal, on a data gap over 30 minutes, or after 4 hours). Events (newest 60 per room) are kept in the integration's store and merged from the analytics history on each hourly relearn. A room is `ready` with `min_warmups` events (confidence high at 8+ events with leave-one-out error up to 15 minutes, medium at 4+, otherwise low). An earlier physics (thermal-constant) fit was dropped because it could not be identified from this house's data.
- **Start.** The goal is HHO's commanded target: the switchpoint's setpoint plus the room's model offset (`air_setpoint - schedule_setpoint`, limited to -1 to +2 K; `schedule_target` and `goal` are both reported). Each event also records a response `delay_minutes` (to the first reading 0.1 K above the start). Pace is the recency-weighted (7-day half-life) median of minutes per kelvin after that delay, and heating time is `delay + pace x climb`. For the next upward switchpoint within the maximum lead the start is the switchpoint minus pace times the climb to target minus tolerance, plus the margin, capped at the maximum lead (the excess is reported as `late_minutes`). With 8 or more events an outdoor term (`a + b * (target - outdoor)`) is used only when `b` is positive and it beats the median on leave-one-out error; outdoor comes from the forecast, else the current reading. The prediction is frozen and journalled (`start_predicted`) when its start time is reached.
- **Boost what-if.** Heating minutes scaled by `((s - 5 - Ta) / (boost_flow - 5 - Ta)) ^ 1.3`, with `s` the room's median supply temperature in past events and `Ta` the mean of current and target temperature, clamped to 0.2–1. Never applied; none when `s` is unknown or not at least 5 K above `Ta`.
- **Stop.** The median slope of the room's demand-off cool-downs that began 17:00–23:00 local. With at least `min_cooldowns` of them and a median slope below -0.02 K/h, heating may stop `coast_drop / |slope|` before a downward switchpoint (capped at the maximum lead); otherwise no stop is predicted ("house holding heat" or "insufficient cool-downs").
- **Scoring.** Closed 120 minutes after the switchpoint (or when the zone setpoint no longer matches the schedule target): a room already at its goal at the switchpoint is `not_needed` and excluded from the rolling scores. Otherwise Evohome's start is the first time before the switchpoint the zone setpoint reached the schedule target, else the switchpoint itself (`evohome_early`); the room's arrival is searched from that start only. Scored: Evohome's lateness, the model's warm-up error (predicted heating minutes from Evohome's start conditions minus actual), an estimate of HHO's own lateness (a lead-0 prediction is judged from when it was made), the lateness a boost flow (default 65 °C) would have had (what-if only, pace part only), and the forecast error. Journalled as `start_scored`; the last 30 are kept.
- **Forecast.** An optional weather entity's hourly forecast (fetched every 30 minutes) replaces the current outdoor reading; a missing or over three hours old forecast falls back to the current reading.

`sensor.home_heating_optimisation_optimum_start` shows `disabled`, `needs_analytics`, `learning` or `ready` with per-room summaries and rolling scores. Each room has `..._<room name>_optimum_start` and `..._optimum_stop` timestamp sensors (e.g. `sensor.home_heating_optimisation_kitchen_optimum_start`) with the lead, target, events, minutes per kelvin, outdoor model use, leave-one-out error, median supply, coast rate, last three events, confidence, the boost what-if and the last score. A start sensor stays `unknown` until the room's next warm-up is within the maximum lead; meanwhile `next_warmup` and `predicting_from` say which warm-up is next and when predicting begins. Learning waits for the analytics history to load after a restart. The control report has an `optimum` block. Scoring in progress is lost on a restart; models and scores are kept.

## Boiler during hot water charges

**Configure -> Boiler during hot water charges** holds two optional aids, both off by default. Saved on its own; it never resets controllers.

- **Anti-cycle override.** Pick a number entity for the boiler's minimum burn period (suggested `number.boiler_burnminperiod`) and a lower value (default 5 minutes, 0-15). When DHW starts HHO remembers the entity's value as the normal one, writes the lower value with `number.set_value`, and writes the normal one back when DHW has been off for 2 minutes. The normal value is stored with a pending-restore flag, so after a restart HHO restores it straight away unless a charge is running; it is re-read whenever it changes outside a charge. Starting needs the boiler in auto and the write guard; restoring needs only the write guard. Each write is confirmed against the entity within 2 minutes, retried once, then shown as `failed` (tried again after 10 minutes). Boiler data: `dhw_anticycle_state`, `dhw_anticycle_normal`, `dhw_anticycle_value`; journal kind `dhw_anticycle`.
- **Hot-cylinder flow.** With `hot_flow` set (70-85 C), a DHW charge whose cylinder reads at least `hot_threshold` (default 48 C), or during a raised DHW schedule session, uses this as the DHW flow target instead of the normal one. It is still limited by the DHW maximum flow and the boiler's own maximum flow entity. `dhw_flow_source` shows which applies. Check the coil rating first.

## Planned morning pre-heat

**Configure -> Planned morning pre-heat** makes a schedule time mean "room warm by then". It is off by default, saved on its own (it never resets controllers) and needs the optimum start predictor enabled (`active` is refused otherwise). Modes: `off`; `shadow` plans and records but writes nothing; `active` starts the rooms.

- **Plan.** For each room's next upward switchpoint the optimum predictor gives a start: dead time + pace x climb to the goal (the next period's commanded air target, `preheat_setpoint`, limited to -1 to +2 K from the schedule), plus the margin. The pace is the learned one scaled by `((s - 5 - Ta) / (plan_flow - 5 - Ta)) ^ 1.3` from the room's median supply `s`, kept within 0.5-2; the dead time is not scaled. The lead is capped at `max_lead_minutes` (default 240); the excess is `late_risk_minutes`. Options: `plan_flow` (default 55), `max_lead_minutes`, and `fullness_low` / `fullness_high` (0.45 / 0.65, the radiator-fullness flow cap below).
- **Hot water.** The next scheduled DHW On time comes from the Evohome cloud water heater (`status.setpoints.next_sp_from` with `next_sp_state` On; the DHW cutoff's cloud entity). A start that would overlap the expected charge (median of the last 10 observed charges, 40 minutes until 3 are seen) moves to the charge's end, and `late_risk_minutes` shows any resulting lateness. While the boiler reports DHW charging a pre-heat waits, but only until the earlier of the expected end of the charge plus 10 minutes and the latest start that still arrives by the switchpoint at the boost flow; then it starts anyway (`dhw_wait_minutes`, `start_reason`, and `late_risk_minutes` recorded). One already running continues.
- **Start.** In `active`, from the planned start the room controller writes `preheat_setpoint` as its normal 60-minute temporary override (refreshed 15 minutes before expiry). Manual holds and open windows still win. The room's decision state is `preheat`. The override is recognised as HHO's own, and at the switchpoint the model setpoint is about the same value, so there is no extra write. The plan latches once started and is kept in the optimum store, so a restart mid warm-up continues. If HHO stops, the override lapses within an hour and Evohome switches at the schedule time.
- **Trajectory.** Expected room temperature is flat through the dead time, then a straight line to the goal over the planned climb. `on_plan_k` is actual minus expected (negative: behind plan).
- **Scoring and journal.** Journal kind `preheat` with events `planned`, `started`, `arrived` and `scored` (an hour after the switchpoint: arrival minutes against the warm-by time, peak and overshoot). In the optimum scores, a setpoint HHO itself wrote is not counted as Evohome starting early.
- **Status.** `sensor.home_heating_optimisation_preheat` shows `off`, `shadow` or `active`; attributes per room: planned start, warm-by time, goal, started, `on_plan_k`, late risk, last arrival and overshoot, plus the expected DHW charge length.
- **Flow during the pre-heat.** While pre-heat is `shadow` or `active` and any room is pre-heating, the boiler's uplift is `min(need, fullness cap)` x the overshoot trim x the return guard, replacing the house-demand uplift (which stays the fallback when no room is pre-heating; never during hot water). Need: `(max flow - base) x clamp(worst kelvin behind plan / 0.5, 0, 1)`, ignoring rooms already done (air >= goal - tolerance - learned coast-up) or on or ahead of plan. Fullness `F = (return - room) / (flow - room)` uses the current flow reading, the fresh return reading and the mean air temperature of rooms calling for heat (else all heated rooms); it is undefined with under 3 K of span, filtered with a 3-minute time constant and only updated while the burner fires (held otherwise). Cap: the full headroom below `fullness_low`, linear to 0 at `fullness_high`; unknown F means no cap. The uplift rises at most 1 K a minute (`rise_k_per_min`) and falls at most the decay rate. In `shadow` the boiler only publishes `preheat_need`, `fullness_cap`, `preheat_uplift`, `preheat_flow` and `preheat_rooms`; `radiator_fullness` is published whenever heating. The `warmup_boost` journal records need, cap and fullness.
- **Coast-up.** Per room, the median of: highest temperature in the 40 minutes after demand ends (first demand end at or after a warm-up's arrival, within 90 minutes) minus the temperature then; excluded for a data gap over 30 minutes or a target drop of 0.3 K. Needs 3 samples, else 0.3 K. Shown as `coast_up_k` on the room's optimum sensor.
- **For the boiler side.** `Optimum.preheat(room_id, now)` returns the room's plan (or None) and `Controls.preheat_rooms()` lists rooms pre-heating now with goal, expected temperature and lag. `Controls.warmup_rooms()` passes the commanded target while a room is pre-heating.

## Warm-up flow boost

**Configure → Warm-up flow boost** raises the boiler flow target while the house is asking for a lot of heat. It is off by default, saved on its own (it never resets controllers to shadow) and applies to the boiler's heating mode only, never to hot water charging.

- **Signal.** Each enabled room's `heat_demand` (0-1 on its primary climate, read only when fresh) weighted by the room's installed radiator output gives the share of the house's capacity being asked for. With fewer than half the rooms reporting there is no boost. `room_equivalents` is the plain sum of demands.
- **Uplift.** Zero below the start share (default 0.15), the full gap to the maximum flow at the full share (default 0.40), linear between. It rises at once and falls no faster than the decay setting (default 1 K per minute). The boost flow is the weather-curve value plus uplift, never above the maximum flow.
- **Modes.** `off`; `shadow` publishes `house_demand`, `room_equivalents`, `warmup_boost` and `boost_flow` and journals `warmup_boost` events without changing what is written; `active` adds the uplift to the written target and fades it out as the filtered return temperature approaches the return ceiling minus 5 K.
- **Report.** The control report has a `warmup_boost` block: mode, current demand share and uplift, minutes in boost today, peak demand share and uplift. Evohome still decides when the boiler fires; a hotter target makes each burn deliver more heat.
- **Overshoot trim.** The uplift is scaled down by the room that has warmed furthest past its commanded air target since the boost began (HHO's room air temperature minus its target, less that room's overshoot when the boost started; baselines are kept in memory for the episode, a room first seen mid-episode is baselined then, and a restart mid-episode re-baselines). Only rooms still calling for heat count. No trim up to 0.3 K added, fading linearly to zero at 0.8 K. A room already over target before the boost does not hold it back. Applied to the target uplift in `shadow` and `active`, so the boost still falls at the decay rate. `boost_overshoot_k`, `boost_overshoot_room`, `boost_overshoot_baseline_k` and `boost_trim` are published, and the report shows the peak overshoot today.
